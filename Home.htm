Welcome

Quantum states, engineered one atomic layer at a time

Our laboratory studies low-dimensional quantum materials and their applications in nanoelectronic devices. We are particularly interested in controlling exotic electronic states in these materials and in the van der Waals heterostructures assembled from them.

We develop and use several carrier-density modulation techniques — ionic-liquid electrical double-layer transistors (EDLTs) and controllable electron-beam doping — together with nanofabrication and low-temperature transport, to explore electric-field-induced metal–insulator transitions, interfacial superconductivity, structural phase transitions and ferromagnetic transitions.

Our aim is to uncover new physical phenomena and to build high-performance, low-power electronic devices from low-dimensional quantum materials.

Research

What we work on

Four threads run through the group, from crystal growth to the measured device.

Low-dimensional quantum materials

Crystals only a few atoms thick host electronic phases with no bulk counterpart. We grow, contact and probe them.

Read more

Van der Waals heterostructures

Stacking different two-dimensional crystals with a chosen twist builds designer potentials and moiré superlattices.

Read more

Carrier-density control

Ionic-liquid EDLTs and focused electron-beam doping let us move a single sample through many electronic phases.

Read more

Low-temperature nanoelectronics

Nanofabricated devices measured at cryogenic temperatures reveal superconductivity, magnetism and quantum transport.
